MeSure — Email Safety Training

Corporate Training E-Learning Interaction Design Interactive Learning

This e-learning mock-up brief is from John Hinchliffe's Instructional Designer Portfolio Masterclass course.

Background

MeSure is a (fictional) US-based home and car insurance company and currently employs 2,000 employees. As part of employee development program, they have been conducting 60-minute mandatory courses and in-person training for leadership.

Needs

There have been new changes. Now, MeSure needs to conduct mandatory compliance training twice a year. We know from previous learning experience feedback that employees don't enjoy current training as it is long, boring, and dull.

On top of that, current learning materials only consist of text and images for 60 minutes with no interactions and a quiz at the end.

Therefore, MeSure wants a new learning design that is interactive, visually appealing, but still follows their corporate branding. They still want the information to be passed to employees, but are open to ways to break it into bite-sized pieces.

Who will learn?

All 2,000 of contact center agents and admin staff will be required to complete this training. They will be taking the lessons on desktop computers, as they can't access the LMS via mobile. The training should include a form of assessment to show people are compliant to learning.

About the project

MeSure Email Safety Training is designed to protect company against email-based threats. As a learning experience designer, I crafted this 5 minutes example of the mandatory course with interactive content, real world examples, and engaging assessments to ensure a practical understanding of email security principles.

The course is structured into concise, easily digestible modules that cover crucial topics such as identifying phishing emails, understanding two-factor authentication, and implementing best practices for email security.

Breaking down the course design

To start the learning, employees (who will be referred as "user" from this point) need to click the "Start" button.

Next, the why is presented. Here, I'm trying to benefit from primacy effect, which is the tendency to remember the first piece of information we encounter. Important key words are highlighted to emphasize the course's urgency.

The text also tries to follow good UX rule, such as maintaining line spacing between 1.3–1.5 for better readability.

Before starting the actual lessons, user will be prompted to answer a mini quiz. This serves as a baseline check to user's prior knowledge and an effort to ensure learning readiness.

There are several ways information is being delivered on this course. Here, user will learn about general structure of phishing email. Besides presenting the key points, I'm also attaching a visual example to better demonstrate what a phishing email might look like.

When user click "show details", a pop-up window will appear. Inside the window, user can hover their cursor over the tooltip to reveal how a part of email can be a suggestion to phishing.

I also use media, such as images and videos, to present information while keeping the user engaged.

For lessons with videos, there is summary or key takeaways to help user get the information without watching the entire videos.

There are four types of assessments I'm trying to incorporate in this course. This variation is intended to keep user engaged without abandoning the quality of assessment. In doing so, learning designers, and in turn the company, are able to understand how much of learning objective is achieved.

I use another interactive way to present information for user to learn. In this section, user can learn how to make sure their email is safe while doing self-reflection using a series of checklist!

Once completed, user can access previous lessons which they might need to repeat.
